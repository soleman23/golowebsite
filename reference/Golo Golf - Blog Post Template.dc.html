How to play a Nassau: the $2 bet that runs every group in America

Front nine, back nine, total match — plus presses. The whole format in five minutes, including the three rules your group is definitely playing differently than the next one.

From the GoLo team August 4, 2026

A Nassau is the default bet in American golf, and almost nobody explains it before the first tee. Somebody says "two-two-two?", everyone nods, and eighteen holes later there's an argument in the parking lot about whether the back nine was still live. Here is the whole thing, start to settle.

NASSAU AT A GLANCE
PLAYERS2, or two teams of two
SCORINGMatch play, hole by hole
THE BETSFront 9, back 9, total 18
TYPICAL STAKE$2 / $5 / $10 a side

It's three bets, not one

That's the only idea you need. A Nassau splits the round into three separate match-play wagers of equal value: the front nine, the back nine, and the overall eighteen. Each is won by whoever is up when that segment ends. Ties push — nobody pays.

Match play means you're counting holes won, not strokes. Lose the 4th by six shots and it costs you exactly one hole, same as losing it by one. This is why the format survives a blow-up: a triple bogey ends the hole, not the round.

SEGMENT RESULT PAYS
Front nine You win 2 up +$2
Back nine You lose 1 down −$2
Total 18 You win 1 up +$2
Settle Net for the day +$2

A $2 Nassau, played straight. Three bets, one number at the end.

$6 Maximum you can lose in a straight $2 Nassau, before anyone presses. That's the whole appeal — the number stays small enough that nobody plays scared, and big enough that a five-footer on 18 still means something.

Presses: the part that gets people in trouble

A press is a brand-new bet, for the same stake, covering only the holes remaining in that segment. It's how the player who's losing buys a way back in. It's also how a $2 Nassau quietly becomes an $18 afternoon.

1 You go 2 down on the front with four holes left. You call the press on the tee.
2 A second $2 match starts on holes 6 through 9. The original front-nine bet keeps running underneath it.
3 Win those four holes 2 up and you've turned a −$2 front into a push plus $2. Lose them and you're out $4 on nine holes.
4 Presses can be pressed. This is where groups lose the thread, and where writing it down stops being optional.
!
SETTLE THIS ON THE FIRST TEE Automatic presses at 2 down, or press only when someone asks? Auto-presses roughly double the money at stake over eighteen holes. Both are fine. Finding out on the 14th which one you're playing is not.

Nobody argues about the golf. They argue about what the bet was.

The three rules your group plays differently

There is no governing body for the Nassau. Every group has house rules, and every group assumes theirs are the standard ones. These are the three that actually cause the parking-lot conversation.

Do strokes count?
A Nassau with handicaps is a different game than one without. Most groups play the low handicap off scratch and give the rest their difference, allocated by the stroke index on the card. If the 6 and the 22 are playing straight up, the 22 is donating.
When does the total 18 close?
Some groups close the overall match the moment it's mathematically decided — 3 up with 2 to play, done. Others play all eighteen because the back-nine bet is still alive anyway. Pick one, because it changes whether 17 and 18 mean anything.
Can you press on 18?
A one-hole press on the last tee is either a fair last chance or a shameless double-or-nothing, depending entirely on who's asking. Decide before somebody needs it.

Settling up

With two players it's one number. With four, in a two-on-two Nassau with a couple of presses in it, you have six or eight small bets resolving in different directions, and somebody is doing arithmetic in a parking lot with a beer in his hand. That's the part that goes wrong — not the golf, not even the rules. The tally.

The fix is boring: agree on the format out loud, write the stake down before the first tee shot, and mark each press when it's called rather than reconstructing it later. Do that and the Nassau is the best bet in golf — three chances to win, a ceiling you can live with, and every hole still mattering to somebody.

THE SHORT VERSION
Three equal bets: front, back, total. Match play. Ties push.
A press is a new bet on the holes that are left, at the same stake.
Decide strokes, auto-presses, and the 18th-tee press before you tee off.
Straight $2 Nassau: $6 is the most anyone can lose. Presses change that.
